My Portfolio

A record of what I can do, what I am still learning, and how I got here.

I would rather finish one page well than ten pages quickly.

Hey I'm Angelito Gerali, I'm a first year BSCS student in Tangub City Global College. Most of what I know about computers I learned the way everyone else did, by breaking something, reading the error message twice, and trying again until the screen agreed with me. I like clean layouts, plain language, and pages that still open on a phone bought three years ago. I am not the fastest coder in the room, but I am the one who checks whether the thing still works when the window is small.

What I work with

HTML & CSS

Semantic markup, flexible layouts, and a stubborn refusal to install a framework for a page that does not need one.

JavaScript

Enough of it to make things move: form checks, a working sign-in, and small animations that respect reduced-motion.

Writing & research

Short sentences, named sources, and the discipline to cut a paragraph I liked if it stopped earning its place.

Careful habits

Readable type, visible focus states, alt text, and testing on a small screen before calling anything finished.

How I got here

First file

The first page I ever wrote

One heading, six colours, and a picture that refused to appear because I had typed its folder name wrong. I thought it was the best page on the internet.

Stylesheets

Learning that one line moves everything

I discovered CSS and spent a month moving boxes around. That was also the month I learned that power without restraint is just clutter.

Now

This Project

Where I am learning that a website is a promise rather than a decoration, and that the small, unglamorous parts are the ones people actually depend on.

What I want to learn next is mostly patience.

I want to understand JavaScript well enough to stop guessing, to read documentation without flinching, and to build something that holds real data without falling apart. I want to learn how design systems keep a hundred pages honest, and how to test my own work the way a stranger would, slowly and without mercy.
